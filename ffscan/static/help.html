Scanning, step by step

  1. Check the status at the top says Ready: photos loaded. Press Refresh after loading photos.
  2. Choose where to save under Save to, and optionally an Album name.
  3. Pick your scan settings and fill in any metadata for the batch.
  4. Press Scan. Photos feed one by one and appear in the grid as each one finishes.
  5. When the feeder is empty the scan ends by itself, unless Keep scanning as I load more photos is on. Press Stop at any time; the photo in progress is ejected and the scan cancelled cleanly.

Settings are remembered between sessions. Change them only when a batch needs something different.

Loading photos

  • Load photos face up, top edge first, pushed gently into the feeder until they stop.
  • Up to 36 photos of 4×6″, 3.5×5″ or 5×7″; 10 photos of 8×10″; one Polaroid or panorama at a time.
  • Smallest photo: 2×2″. Widest: 8.5″. Longest: 36″ at 300 or 600 dpi.
  • Stacks can mix sizes. Put the largest at the back.
  • Use the carrier sheet for torn, curled or very thin photos.

Where photos are saved

Save to
The folder on this Mac where photos go. Type a path or press Choose… for a folder picker. Each batch goes into a subfolder named after the Album, or today's date (e.g. 2026-10-30) if no album is set.
File names
By default a photo is named after the scan time plus its number in the stack, e.g. 20261030-185940_001.jpg. Type a prefix under File names to use your own, e.g. Grandma gives Grandma_001.jpg, Grandma_002.jpg… You can include {album} (the album name) and {date} (today, YYYY-MM-DD). Numbering continues after the highest number already in the album, so scanning another stack never overwrites a file. Backs end in _back.jpg, enhanced copies in _enhanced.jpg.

Scan settings

Resolution
300 dpi is right for most prints. A 4×6″ photo becomes about 1800×1200 pixels, enough for a sharp 4×6″ reprint or any screen. 600 dpi is the scanner's maximum true detail: use it for small photos you may enlarge, or to crop in later. It's about twice as slow and files are four times bigger. 150 dpi is for quick previews or email.
Colour
Colour for everything, including faded colour prints. Greyscale makes smaller black-and-white files, good for B&W originals.
Sides
The scanner reads both sides of every photo in one pass, so this only decides what is saved. Picture only saves the image. Picture and back also keeps the back, useful for dates and notes written there. Back only saves only the backs.
Skip backs with no writing
With backs being saved, throws away backs that have nothing written on them. Faint paper-maker watermarks (Kodak, Fuji, DNP) and printed lab codes count as blank. Handwriting, stamps and stickers are kept.
Crop to photo
The scanner always captures its full 8.5″ width, so a photo sits on a grey background. This trims the background so the file is just the photo. Turn it off to keep the full scan.
Straighten (deskew)
The scanner straightens photos that fed in at a slight angle. The corners it fills in are trimmed by Crop to photo.
Auto-rotate (photos with faces)
Uses Apple's face detection to turn photos with people upright, whichever way they went in. Photos without people (landscapes, rooms, pets) are left as scanned. Use the ⟲ ⟳ buttons on those. Rotation only changes an orientation tag in the file, so image quality is untouched. Every photo app respects it.
Also save an enhanced copy
Saves a second file (…_enhanced.jpg) with the fixes ticked below it. The original is always kept, so you can compare and delete whichever you don't want. The Enhance button on each photo applies the same fixes to photos scanned earlier. Save enhanced copies decides where they go: next to the original, in an Enhanced subfolder of each album, or in another folder of your choice (with the same album subfolders). Enhanced copies show in the grid wherever they are, and cloud uploads keep the same folder layout.
  • Remove red-eye: Apple's face detection finds the eyes, and only strongly red pixels inside them are turned neutral. Skin and everything else stay untouched.
  • Restore faded colours: old prints fade unevenly and drift to a red or magenta cast. Each colour channel is stretched back to full range, which restores contrast and neutral colours together.
  • Auto brightness, contrast & saturation: measures each photo and nudges it toward a natural look. Dark photos get lighter, flat ones more contrast, dull ones more colour, plus slight sharpening. Photos that already look right change very little.
Double-feed detection
Stops the scan when two photos stick together and go through at once, which would otherwise lose one of them. Normal suits most prints. Sensitive catches more but may stop on thick, curled or stuck-on-backing photos. Off never stops; use it for photos that keep stopping a scan for no reason.
JPEG quality
How much the files are compressed, from 50 to 100. 90 looks identical to the original for prints. Higher values make bigger files with little visible gain.
Keep scanning as I load more photos
When the feeder empties, the app waits and scans the next stack as soon as you load it, so you can work through a box of photos without touching the computer. Press Stop when you're done.

Metadata

Metadata is written into each photo file (EXIF, IPTC and XMP), so it travels with the photo to Apple Photos, Google Photos, Lightroom, a USB stick, or the cloud.

Album
Album name. Also the subfolder name under Save to and in the cloud.
Date taken
When the photos were taken: 1985, 1985-07 or 1985-07-14. Missing month or day becomes 01. Photo apps then sort the scans by when they were taken, not when you scanned them.
Place
Where they were taken.
People
Names separated by commas. Stored in the standard "person in image" field so photo apps can search by name.
Tags
Keywords separated by commas.
Description
A caption stored with every photo of the batch.

Batch metadata vs. editing later

Fields in the side panel are applied to every photo scanned while they are filled in, so clear them before the next batch. To change photos afterwards, click their thumbnails to select them, then press Edit metadata…. In that dialog, empty fields are left unchanged, and people and tags are added to the ones already there.

Duplicates

The app recognises a print you've already scanned, even at another resolution, crop or orientation, by comparing a visual fingerprint with every photo in the Save to folder. Later scans of the same print get a Duplicate badge (hover to see which photo it repeats). The first scan of each print is never marked.

  • Select duplicates selects every marked photo; check them, then Delete selected moves them to the Trash.
  • The Duplicates setting under Scan can instead skip saving a print that's already there, or turn the check off.
  • Two different photos of the same scene taken seconds apart can look alike; they were told apart correctly in testing, but review before deleting.

Upload progress

While photos upload, a progress bar above the photos shows how many are done, which file is uploading, and any failures. Each photo's badge also turns from Uploading… to Uploaded.

The Activity panel at the bottom of the window shows what's happening: photos saved, backs skipped, uploads and errors. Minimise it with ▾ to a single line showing the latest message; it stays minimised next time.

The settings on the left are grouped into sections. Click a section's title to open or close it; closed sections show a one-line summary of their current values.

Cloud upload

Connect an account once, then every photo can upload as soon as it's saved. Everything happens in this app; no Terminal needed.

Connecting an account

  1. Under Cloud upload, press + Connect account… and pick a service.
  2. Google Drive, Google Photos, Dropbox, OneDrive: press Sign in. A sign-in page opens in your browser. Approve access, then come back; the account appears in the list.
  3. Amazon S3 (or S3-compatible such as Backblaze B2, Wasabi, Cloudflare R2): enter the access key, secret key and region (plus the endpoint for non-AWS services), then press Connect.

Google Drive asks only for access to files this app creates, so it can't see or change anything else in your Drive.

Accounts
Connected accounts. Choose the one that receives uploads with its round button. Test checks the connection; Remove disconnects it (photos already uploaded stay in the cloud).
Upload each photo automatically
Uploads every saved photo, including enhanced copies, as soon as it's ready, after AI analysis if that's on. Failed uploads are retried 3 times; failures show in red in the log. You can also select photos and press Upload.
Folder in the cloud
Top folder in that account. Press Browse… to pick a folder or create a new one. The browser also opens right after you connect an account. Photos land in <folder>/<album or date>/. For Amazon S3, start with the bucket: my-bucket/FastFoto. Google Photos ignores this: each album becomes a Google Photos album.

If Google refuses the sign-in

The sign-in for Google Drive and Google Photos uses a shared app ID from rclone, the upload tool inside this app, which Google is retiring during 2026. If Google shows an error instead of the consent screen:

  1. In the Google Cloud console open APIs & Services → Credentials → Create credentials → OAuth client ID, type Desktop app. Enable the Google Drive API (or Photos Library API) for the project.
  2. In Connect account → Google Drive, open Use my own Google client ID, paste the client ID and secret, and sign in again.

AI analysis (Claude)

When Analyse photos with Claude is on, every scanned picture is sent to Claude, Anthropic's AI model. This happens in the background while scanning continues. Claude:

  • Turns the photo upright, including landscapes, rooms and other photos without faces. Photos with faces keep the on-device face-based rotation, which is exact.
  • Writes a caption and tags. It never guesses people's names.
  • Estimates when the photo was taken from clothes, cars, print style and colour. A year goes into the date field; a decade becomes a tag like c. 1980s.
  • Guesses the place when it's recognisable.
  • Reads the back: handwriting, stamps and dates, even when you don't save backs. A date written on the back wins over its estimate.

It only fills empty fields. Anything you typed in the batch metadata is kept. To analyse photos scanned earlier, select them and press Analyse.

One-time setup: Anthropic API (default)

  1. Create an API key at console.anthropic.com → API Keys. Usage is billed to that account.
  2. Paste it under AI analysis → API key and press Save. The app checks the key with Anthropic and stores it in this Mac's Keychain, never in a file. Remove deletes it again.
  3. Tick Analyse photos with Claude.

Alternative: Google Vertex AI

  1. gcloud auth application-default login in Terminal.
  2. In the Google Cloud console, open Vertex AI → Model Garden, find Claude Opus 5.5 and press Enable. New projects also need a quota increase (IAM & Admin → Quotas, filter anthropic-claude-opus).
  3. Choose Service → Google Vertex AI and enter your project ID.

Privacy: photos leave your Mac only while this option is on, and only go to the service you chose.

Scanner connection

Address (under Advanced)
Leave it empty and the app finds the scanner on your network automatically. If that fails, type the scanner's network name (e.g. EPSONXXXXXX.local) or its IP address. Hold the scanner's Wi-Fi button to print or show its network status, or check your router's device list.
Status line
Shows whether the scanner is ready, scanning, out of photos or jammed. Press Refresh to check again. Model, serial number and firmware appear under Address once connected.

Only one app can use the scanner at a time. Quit Epson FastFoto, Epson ScanSmart and Epson Scan 2 while using this app.

Troubleshooting

Message or problemWhat to do
Cannot reach scannerCheck the scanner is on and its Wi-Fi light is steady. Try its IP address under Advanced → Address. Wait a few seconds after waking it from sleep, then Refresh.
Feeder is emptyPush the photos in until they stop, face up. Then Refresh.
Paper jamOpen the scanner cover, remove the photo, close the cover, then scan again. Photos already saved are kept.
Scanner refused lockAnother program is using the scanner: quit Epson's software and try again.
Scan stops on a photo that isn't doubledSet Double-feed detection to Off for that photo, or press the scanner's double-feed skip button.
Grey border left around a photoVery dark or grey-edged photos can confuse cropping. Untouched originals of the last 300 scans are kept in ~/Library/Caches/FF680W/raw.
Photo is sideways or upside downUse ⟲ ⟳. Auto-rotate only works on photos with faces.
Uploads failRun rclone lsd <remote>: in Terminal to check the account still works. Google Drive may need rclone config reconnect <remote>:.
